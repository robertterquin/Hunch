# Product Principles

## Product

Hunch is an OJT and internship safety tool for students. It gives students a second opinion before they apply by analyzing pasted posts, public listing pages, and recruiter messages for scam signals.

## Target User

Students who are actively hunting for their OJT or internship placement.

They often discover opportunities through Facebook groups, Messenger, LinkedIn, school pages, email, group chats, or referrals. They may be under deadline pressure and may not know how to verify whether a post is legitimate.

## Product Promise

Hunch helps students quickly answer: "Does this OJT post look safe enough to apply to?"

The app should not claim that a post is definitely real or fake. It should provide an explainable risk assessment and practical next steps.

## Core Principles

1. Start with the main tool.
   The analyzer is the first thing users see. No long marketing intro should block the core action.

2. Explain every warning.
   A risk score is useful only when students understand why the score exists.

3. Use student-friendly language.
   Avoid legal, cybersecurity, or HR jargon unless it is explained clearly.

4. Guide, do not scare.
   The tone should be calm and protective. Hunch should help students make better decisions, not panic.

5. Be transparent about uncertainty.
   The app gives a risk estimate based on signals. It should never guarantee that a listing is safe.

6. Respect privacy.
   OJT posts, public source URLs, names, emails, and recruiter messages may contain sensitive details. Only store what the user chooses to save.

7. Make verification actionable.
   Every result should suggest what the student can do next before applying.

## Product Boundaries

Hunch can:

- Analyze pasted OJT or internship posts.
- Analyze readable text from public listing pages.
- Detect common scam indicators.
- Explain red flags.
- Generate a pre-application checklist.
- Save analysis history for signed-in users.

Hunch should not:

- Guarantee that an internship is legitimate.
- Replace school placement office guidance.
- Publicly accuse a company or person of fraud.
- Fetch private pages or store extracted page content without user intent.
- Ask users for unnecessary personal documents.
